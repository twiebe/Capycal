# Settings

## First start

If the config file doesn't exist when the app starts, or holds nothing but blank lines and comments, the app writes the [starter config](../cli/config-file.md#starter-config) there (creating the directory, keeping existing comments below it) and logs "starter config written". Every setting in it is commented out, so the app greets you with a small "Welcome to Capycal" window (once per start): it asks whether your calendars are in Calendar.app or in a CalDAV account (and adds the account), then for the source, the target (in another account added right there, if need be) and what to copy, one question at a time, with dots showing the step and **Cancel**, **Back** and **Continue** at the bottom (Return continues, Escape cancels). At the end it says the sync runs; **Open Settings** shows the full editor, **Done** closes the window. Sync Now stays disabled until there is one.

## The window

**Settings…** opens a window (as large as you left it, on the entry you looked at last) with your syncs and accounts on the left (a disabled sync has a grey icon, one whose last sync failed an orange one) and the selected one on the right. **Add Sync…** below the syncs and **Add Account…** below the accounts add one. At the bottom of the sidebar is the version of Capycal. Under a sync's name, a line says how it is doing: "Synced 2 min ago: 3 new, 1 removed", "Syncing now…", or why the last sync failed. The bar at the bottom says when there are unsaved changes and holds **Revert** and **Save**; a dot after the entry's name in the list marks them too. **Cmd+S** saves, **Cmd+N** adds a sync and **Cmd+Delete** deletes the entry shown (on Linux with Ctrl).

**Add Sync…** starts the same assistant in a small window over Settings (a sheet on macOS), with one question per step: the source calendar, the target calendar (the source and calendars you may only read are greyed out) what the copies show: **Busy blocker** (only the time, titled "Busy (Work)" after the source calendar), **Title and time**, **Title and details** (title, notes, location and reminders, like **Title, notes, location, reminders** under **Keep**), each card with a preview of a copied event, or **Custom**, and last its name, proposed as `work-to-blockers`. **Create** saves the sync and shows it in the editor below, where you can change everything. A **Custom** sync starts switched off (`enabled = false`), so it copies nothing until you set it up and switch it on. **Cancel** (or closing it) returns to Settings as it was. **Set Up in Editor** leaves the assistant for the editor with what you picked so far, for those who rather fill in the form. A right click on a sync in the list offers **Duplicate**: a new, unsaved copy named `<sync>-copy`. Calendar.app's calendars are listed under their account (iCloud, Exchange, ...), accounts and calendars in alphabetical order. A calendar in a CalDAV account that is not set up yet: **Add Another Account…** (**Add CalDAV Account…** while there is none) below the calendars of the source or target step adds the account in the assistant and comes back to that step with its calendars, for example a second server for the target of a CalDAV to CalDAV sync, or a CalDAV target for a Calendar.app source. **Add Account…** in the sidebar adds one outside the assistant. **Add Calendar Feed…** below the calendars of the first step shows a **Feed address** field: paste a calendar's iCal address there to copy from it.

A **sync** shows its name at the top (click it to rename; Esc or a click beside it ends editing) with its on/off switch at the right (off is `enabled = false`: configured but stopped), and below a sentence saying what it does, such as "Copies events from Calendar into Family every 5 min." The step you work in is outlined in the accent color. Below, the sync is drawn as the steps an event takes, from top to bottom:

- **From**: the source calendar, how often it is checked (**Check every**: 1, 5 or 15 minutes, or **Custom** for any other, such as `10m` or `2h`) and which of its **Events** are copied: **All**, or a **Range** of days back and ahead of today (see [Event window](../concepts/syncs.md#event-window)). A Calendar.app source starts with a range of 30 days back and 365 ahead, since its All reads 20 years back and 4 years ahead; any other source starts with All. Below a range of a CalDAV calendar or a feed, a note says that a recurring event is copied as a whole series if it might have an occurrence in it. The calendar menus list the calendars of every account, found on the server when the window opens, and the calendars of Calendar.app. Without a connection they show the error and the calendars already in the config; **Refresh** next to them asks the servers again. **Add Calendar Feed…** at the end of the menu reads a calendar from its iCal address instead, such as Google's secret address: paste it into **Feed address** below, and the feed is named after the site ("google"). Feeds you added are listed under **Feeds**, and **Feed address** shows and changes the address of the one chosen; an address set in the config file through `env`, `file`, `command` or `keychain` is not shown. See [Calendar feeds](../concepts/calendars.md#calendar-feeds-ical-address).
- **Keep**: the content copied on top of times, recurrence, status and free/busy: **Only the time**, **Title**, **Title, notes, location, reminders**, or **Custom**, which shows the fields as checkboxes and others (such as `X-CUSTOM`) as text.
- One step per **transform**, in the order they run, each numbered and as a sentence ("Set SUMMARY to "Busy""). Click one to edit its fields in it (**Field** suggests common iCal fields such as `LOCATION` and takes any other name too, always in upper case) and its conditions: **Add Condition** adds one, which reads **Only if** (**If** for **Skip events**, which starts with one and keeps at least one) and then **if**, with what it looks at (**Title**, **Location**, **Notes**, **Other field**, **Start** with one checkbox per weekday and **From** / **Until**, **Event** for all-day), its test and its text, arrows to move it up or down, **Remove**, and a handle (≡) to drag it to another place; the **and** / **or** switch between two conditions says whether both have to match or one is enough, and conditions joined by **or** share a box, **One of these** (a condition dropped inside it joins it, one dropped right above or below it stays out) (see [Conditions](../concepts/transforms.md#conditions)); drag it by its handle (≡) to another place, the bin removes it. The **+** between two steps adds a transform right there, each named with a line saying what it does; below the transforms its menu offers **Common steps** that come filled in: hide the details (title "Busy"), travel time (30 min before and after), travel time only for events with a location, skip weekends, skip outside working hours (Mon-Fri before 8:00 and from 18:00) and a reminder 10 min before. **Cmd+Z** undoes adding, moving or removing a step. Without a mouse, a right click on a step (VoiceOver: its context menu) offers **Move Up**, **Move Down** and **Remove**, and **Cmd+Option+Up** / **Down** moves the open step (on Linux Ctrl+Alt). A field with a mistake (an invalid regex, a time like 25:00) shows it below the field once you leave it, not while you type. While something keeps Save disabled, the bar at the bottom says what, with a warning sign, for example "Set field (step 1): fill in Field and Value".
- **Into**: the target calendar. A calendar the account may only read, and a feed, is greyed out.
- **Advanced settings** below: **Parallel writes** of a CalDAV target (`concurrency` of the target calendar, default 2, shared by every sync writing into it), and, for a saved sync, **Troubleshooting**: **Full Sync Now** compares all copies with the source and fixes the ones that differ, as Capycal does by itself once a day (the status line says "Full sync running…" meanwhile, and the bar at the bottom shows how far it got, as it does for the full sync that follows a save), **Remove Synced Events…** removes this sync's copies from the target, after asking, and switches the sync off, **Remove All Events…** empties the target calendar (see below).

Right of the steps, **One event on its way** shows a sample meeting (Tuesday 9:00 to 10:00, with a place, notes and a reminder) and the copy this sync would make of it, through the same fields and transforms the sync uses; it follows every change before you save. **Next event** above it reads the source's next event and shows that one instead of the sample (again each time you choose it). Picking a calendar that is not in the config yet adds a `[calendars.<name>]` entry for it when you save; a calendar no sync uses any more is removed after asking.

**Save** writes the config, keeping your comments and the order of everything else, and the syncs restart with it. Renaming a sync or changing its target first removes the events it copied into the old target, after asking, and then copies the source again; otherwise the old copies would stay behind without a sync that owns them. Changing what a sync copies (source, fields, transforms) makes its next cycle a full sync, so the copies already made get the new content. **Delete** (at the bottom left) removes a sync together with its copies, or with **Keep Copies** leaves them in the target. While copies are removed, the bar at the bottom counts them ("Removing the synced events of \"work-to-home\"… 120 of 340") next to a progress bar.

An **account** has a name, the server URL, the user name and the password. A password you type is stored in the Keychain (service `app.capycal`, the account's name) and the config only says `password = { keychain = "<name>" }`. A password from an environment variable, a file or a command stays as it is until you type a new one. When macOS would ask before Capycal reads a Keychain password (after an update to a build with a new signature), the app first says why and which accounts it reads, and starts the syncs once you close that note; choose **Always Allow** in the macOS prompt so it does not come back. **Test Connection** looks up the account's calendars with what you entered. Below the box, the page lists the syncs that use the account; **Delete** removes it once none does.

If the config file changes while you have unsaved changes to the same sync or account, a yellow **Changed on disk** banner offers **Reload** (drop your changes) or **Overwrite** (keep them; Save writes them). Without unsaved changes the window just shows the new state. Closing the window or choosing another entry with unsaved changes asks first.

What each setting does, with its key in the config: [Calendars and accounts](../concepts/calendars.md), [Syncs](../concepts/syncs.md), [Transforms](../concepts/transforms.md), [Full sync](../concepts/full-sync.md).

## General

**General** at the top of the list holds the app's own settings: **Start Capycal at login**, the **Log level** (written as `[daemon] log_level`; a filter written by hand shows as "Custom") with **Show Log**, and the **Config file** with **Open in Editor…** and **Show in Finder**. When something keeps the config from running, General turns orange and starts with **Needs attention**: the mistake in the config file (where it is and what is wrong) with **Open in Editor…**, another Capycal process holding the config with **Reload Config**, or a failed removal of synced events with **Show Log**. Settings also opens when the config file cannot be read at all, then with only General, and shows everything once the file is fixed. **Open in Editor…** writes the starter config if the file is missing and opens it in the app for `.toml` files, or in the default text editor. Saving it reloads the syncs. Settings and your own edits can be mixed: Settings keeps your comments and only replaces the entries it changes. To find calendar URLs and identifiers for hand-written entries, use `capycal --discover` and `capycal --discover-eventkit` (see [CLI reference](../cli/reference.md#discover)).

### Updates

On macOS, **Updates** on General has **Check for updates automatically** and **Check Now**. With the switch on, Capycal looks for a new release on GitHub once a day and shows a window when there is one:

- **View Release on GitHub** opens the release page; the window stays open.
- **Update Now** downloads the new version, checks it against the release's checksum, replaces `Capycal.app` and starts the new version. Settings that are not saved are lost. macOS may then ask again for calendar access and, for CalDAV accounts, for the Keychain, since the app is not signed with an Apple Developer ID. If the update fails, for example because Capycal is not allowed to write to its folder, the installed version stays and the window says why.
- **Remind Me Later** shows the window again in three days at the earliest; closing the window does the same.
- **Skip This Version** stops the reminders for this version. A newer one is shown again.

**Check Now** looks at once, also with the switch off and for a skipped version, and says below the button whether Capycal is up to date. An app installed with [Homebrew](../installation.md#homebrew) starts with the switch off, and its window has no **Update Now**: it shows `brew upgrade --cask capycal-app` instead. The Linux app has no update check; its packages update it.
